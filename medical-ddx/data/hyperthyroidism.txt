Hyperthyroidism

Overview:
Hyperthyroidism is a condition characterized by excessive production and secretion of thyroid hormones, leading to increased metabolic rate and various systemic effects.

Pathophysiology:
- Increased T4 and T3 production
- Suppressed TSH (primary hyperthyroidism)
- Increased metabolic rate
- Enhanced protein catabolism
- Cardiovascular stimulation
- Sympathetic nervous system activation

Types:
Primary Hyperthyroidism:
- Thyroid gland overactivity
- Low TSH, elevated T4/T3
- Most common type

Secondary Hyperthyroidism:
- Pituitary adenoma (TSH-secreting)
- Very rare

Clinical Presentation:
Common Symptoms:
- Palpitations and tachycardia
- Weight loss despite increased appetite
- Heat intolerance and sweating
- Nervousness and anxiety
- Tremor
- Fatigue and weakness
- Insomnia
- Frequent bowel movements

Physical Signs:
- Tachycardia or atrial fibrillation
- Systolic hypertension
- Warm, moist skin
- Fine tremor
- Hyperreflexia
- Proximal muscle weakness
- Eye signs (in Graves' disease)

Etiology:
Graves' Disease:
- Most common cause (80-85%)
- Autoimmune condition
- TSI (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin)
- Diffuse thyroid enlargement
- Eye disease (ophthalmopathy)

Toxic Multinodular Goiter:
- Multiple functioning nodules
- Common in elderly
- Iodine-induced in iodine-deficient areas

Toxic Adenoma:
- Single functioning nodule
- Autonomous function
- Suppressed surrounding tissue

Thyroiditis:
- Subacute (de Quervain's)
- Silent (lymphocytic)
- Postpartum thyroiditis
- Transient hyperthyroidism

Iodine-induced:
- Contrast agents
- Amiodarone
- Iodine supplements

Exogenous Thyroid Hormone:
- Factitious thyrotoxicosis
- Excessive levothyroxine dosing

Diagnosis:
Laboratory Tests:
TSH:
- Suppressed (<0.1 mIU/L)
- First-line screening test
- Most sensitive indicator

Free T4 and T3:
- Elevated in overt hyperthyroidism
- T3 may be elevated alone (T3 toxicosis)
- Confirms diagnosis

Thyroid Antibodies:
- TSI or TRAb (Graves' disease)
- Anti-TPO antibodies

Thyroglobulin:
- Elevated in thyroiditis
- Low in factitious thyrotoxicosis

Imaging:
Radioactive Iodine Uptake:
- Increased in Graves' disease and toxic nodules
- Decreased in thyroiditis
- Helps determine etiology

Thyroid Scan:
- Diffuse uptake (Graves')
- Hot nodules (toxic adenoma/multinodular)
- Cold areas (thyroiditis)

Ultrasound:
- Assess thyroid structure
- Evaluate nodules
- Doppler flow assessment

Treatment:
Antithyroid Drugs:
Methimazole:
- Preferred agent
- Once daily dosing
- Lower side effect profile
- Teratogenic in first trimester

Propylthiouracil (PTU):
- Preferred in pregnancy (first trimester)
- Three times daily dosing
- Higher risk of liver toxicity

Beta-blockers:
- Propranolol most commonly used
- Symptom control
- All patients initially
- Contraindicated in asthma

Radioactive Iodine (RAI):
- Definitive treatment
- Contraindicated in pregnancy/nursing
- Results in hypothyroidism
- Single dose usually effective

Surgery:
- Near-total or total thyroidectomy
- Large goiters
- Malignancy suspected
- Failed medical therapy
- Patient preference

Treatment Selection:
Graves' Disease:
- Antithyroid drugs first-line
- RAI for definitive treatment
- Surgery if large goiter

Toxic Nodular Disease:
- RAI preferred
- Surgery for large nodules
- Antithyroid drugs for preparation

Thyroiditis:
- Beta-blockers for symptoms
- Self-limiting condition
- Monitor for hypothyroid phase

Monitoring:
- Free T4 and T3 every 4-6 weeks
- Adjust antithyroid drug dosing
- Monitor for side effects
- CBC with differential
- Liver function tests

Complications:
Thyroid Storm:
- Life-threatening emergency
- Fever, tachycardia, delirium
- High-dose antithyroid drugs
- Beta-blockers, steroids
- Supportive care

Graves' Ophthalmopathy:
- Eyelid retraction
- Proptosis
- Diplopia
- Optic neuropathy
- Selenium supplementation
- Steroids for severe cases

Cardiac Complications:
- Atrial fibrillation
- Heart failure
- Increased cardiovascular mortality

Bone Effects:
- Accelerated bone loss
- Increased fracture risk
- Osteoporosis

Drug Side Effects:
- Agranulocytosis (rare but serious)
- Hepatotoxicity
- Rash
- Arthritis

Pregnancy and Hyperthyroidism:
- PTU in first trimester
- Methimazole in second/third trimester
- Monitor closely
- Fetal thyroid function
- Neonatal complications possible

Subclinical Hyperthyroidism:
- Low TSH, normal T4/T3
- May be asymptomatic
- Increased cardiovascular risk
- Treatment controversial
- Consider if TSH <0.1

Key Points:
- Common endocrine disorder
- Graves' disease most common cause
- TSH suppression is hallmark
- Multiple treatment options available
- Beta-blockers for immediate symptom control
- Antithyroid drugs, RAI, or surgery for definitive treatment
- Monitor for complications
- Special considerations in pregnancy
