Hypertension: Comprehensive Clinical Management Guide

DEFINITION AND CLASSIFICATION
Hypertension, commonly known as high blood pressure, is a chronic medical condition characterized by persistently elevated arterial blood pressure. It is often called the "silent killer" because it typically has no symptoms until complications develop.

2017 AHA/ACC Blood Pressure Classification:
- Normal: <120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120-129/<80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: 130-139/80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: ≥140/90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: >180/120 mmHg

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- Affects approximately 45% of US adults
- Prevalence increases with age
- More common in men <65 years, women >65 years
- Higher prevalence in African Americans
- Leading risk factor for cardiovascular disease and stroke
- Contributes to over 400,000 deaths annually in the US

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Blood pressure is determined by cardiac output and systemic vascular resistance:
BP = CO × SVR

Multiple systems regulate blood pressure:

1. Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS):
   - Renin released from juxtaglomerular cells
   - Converts angiotensinogen to angiotensin I
   - ACE converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II
   - Angiotensin II causes vasoconstriction and aldosterone release
   - Aldosterone promotes sodium and water retention

2. Sympathetic Nervous System:
   - Increased norepinephrine release
   - Enhanced cardiac contractility and heart rate
   - Peripheral vasoconstriction
   - Renal sodium retention

3. Endothelial Function:
   - Nitric oxide (NO) promotes vasodilation
   - Endothelin-1 causes vasoconstriction
   - Imbalance contributes to hypertension

4. Renal Function:
   - Kidney's role in sodium and volume regulation
   - Pressure natriuresis mechanism
   - Chronic kidney disease both cause and consequence

TYPES OF HYPERTENSION

Primary (Essential) Hypertension (90-95%):
- No identifiable cause
- Multifactorial etiology
- Genetic and environmental factors
- Progressive condition developing over years

Secondary Hypertension (5-10%):
Common causes include:
1. Renal Disease:
   - Chronic kidney disease
   - Renovascular disease (renal artery stenosis)
   - Glomerulonephritis
   - Polycystic kidney disease

2. Endocrine Disorders:
   - Primary aldosteronism (Conn's syndrome)
   - Pheochromocytoma
   - Cushing's syndrome
   - Hyperthyroidism/hypothyroidism
   - Hyperparathyroidism

3. Vascular Causes:
   - Coarctation of aorta
   - Aortic regurgitation

4. Drug-Induced:
   - NSAIDs
   - Decongestants
   - Stimulants (amphetamines, cocaine)
   - Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine)
   - Oral contraceptives
   - Corticosteroids
   - Antidepressants (SNRIs)

5. Other:
   - Sleep apnea
   - Pregnancy-related (preeclampsia)

CLINICAL PRESENTATION

Symptoms (Usually Absent):
Most patients are asymptomatic until complications develop.

Possible symptoms with severe hypertension:
- Headaches (typically occipital, morning)
- Dizziness
- Blurred vision
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Nosebleeds (rare)

Hypertensive Crisis Symptoms:
- Severe headache
- Nausea and vomiting
- Confusion
- Visual disturbances
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Anxiety

Physical Examination Findings:
- Elevated blood pressure on multiple readings
- Retinal changes (AV nicking, hemorrhages, exudates, papilledema)
- S4 gallop
- Left ventricular heave
- Peripheral edema
- Bruits (carotid, renal, femoral)
- Signs of secondary causes

RISK FACTORS

Non-Modifiable:
- Age (risk increases with age)
- Gender (men <65, women >65)
- Race/ethnicity (higher in African Americans)
- Family history
- Genetic factors

Modifiable:
- Obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²)
- Physical inactivity
- Excessive sodium intake (>2300 mg/day)
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Tobacco use
- Diabetes mellitus
- Dyslipidemia
- Chronic kidney disease
- Sleep apnea
- Stress

DIAGNOSTIC EVALUATION

Blood Pressure Measurement:
- Proper technique essential
- Appropriate cuff size
- Patient seated, feet flat, arm supported
- Multiple readings on separate occasions
- Out-of-office BP monitoring recommended
  * Home BP monitoring
  * Ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM)

Initial Laboratory Tests:
- Complete blood count (CBC)
- Basic metabolic panel (electrolytes, creatinine, BUN)
- Fasting glucose or HbA1c
- Lipid profile
- Urinalysis
- Spot urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio
- Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)

Additional Tests Based on Clinical Suspicion:
- Echocardiogram (if clinical evidence of heart disease)
- 24-hour urine catecholamines (pheochromocytoma)
- Plasma aldosterone-to-renin ratio (primary aldosteronism)
- Renal imaging (renovascular disease)
- Sleep study (sleep apnea)

COMPLICATIONS

Cardiovascular:
- Coronary artery disease
- Myocardial infarction
- Left ventricular hypertrophy
- Heart failure
- Atrial fibrillation
- Sudden cardiac death

Cerebrovascular:
- Stroke (ischemic and hemorrhagic)
- Transient ischemic attack
- Vascular dementia

Renal:
- Chronic kidney disease
- End-stage renal disease
- Hypertensive nephrosclerosis

Vascular:
- Peripheral arterial disease
- Aortic aneurysm
- Aortic dissection

Retinal:
- Hypertensive retinopathy
- Vision loss

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES

Treatment Goals:
- <130/80 mmHg for most adults
- <130/80 mmHg for adults with cardiovascular disease or 10-year ASCVD risk ≥10%
- Individualized based on patient factors and comorbidities

Lifestyle Modifications (First-line for all patients):

1. DASH Diet:
   - Emphasizes fruits, vegetables, whole grains
   - Low-fat dairy products
   - Lean proteins
   - Nuts and legumes
   - Low in saturated fat and sodium

2. Sodium Restriction:
   - <2300 mg/day (optimal <1500 mg/day)
   - Average reduction: 2-8 mmHg

3. Weight Management:
   - Target BMI 18.5-24.9 kg/m²
   - Weight loss of 2.2 lbs reduces BP by ~1 mmHg

4. Physical Activity:
   - At least 150 minutes/week moderate aerobic activity
   - Muscle-strengthening activities 2+ days/week
   - Average reduction: 2-8 mmHg

5. Alcohol Moderation:
   - Men: ≤2 drinks/day
   - Women: ≤1 drink/day
   - Average reduction: 2-4 mmHg

6. Smoking Cessation:
   - Acute increase in BP with smoking
   - Long-term cardiovascular risk reduction

PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT

First-Line Antihypertensive Agents:

1. ACE Inhibitors:
   Examples: Lisinopril, enalapril, captopril
   Mechanism: Block conversion of angiotensin I to II
   Benefits: Cardiovascular protection, renal protection
   Side effects: Dry cough (10%), hyperkalemia, angioedema (rare)
   Contraindications: Pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis

2. Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs):
   Examples: Losartan, valsartan, olmesartan
   Mechanism: Block angiotensin II receptors
   Benefits: Similar to ACE inhibitors, lower cough incidence
   Side effects: Hyperkalemia, angioedema (very rare)
   Contraindications: Pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis

3. Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs):
   Dihydropyridines: Amlodipine, nifedipine, felodipine
   Mechanism: Block calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle
   Benefits: No metabolic effects, good for isolated systolic hypertension
   Side effects: Peripheral edema, gingival hyperplasia, constipation

   Non-dihydropyridines: Verapamil, diltiazem
   Mechanism: Block calcium channels in heart and vessels
   Benefits: Heart rate control
   Side effects: Bradycardia, heart block, constipation

4. Thiazide and Thiazide-like Diuretics:
   Examples: Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ), chlorthalidone, indapamide
   Mechanism: Inhibit sodium reabsorption in distal tubule
   Benefits: Cardiovascular outcome data, low cost
   Side effects: Hypokalemia, hyperuricemia, hyperglycemia, hyponatremia

Treatment Algorithm:

Stage 1 Hypertension:
- Lifestyle modifications × 3-6 months
- If BP remains elevated, start single agent
- Consider combination therapy if BP >20/10 mmHg above goal

Stage 2 Hypertension:
- Lifestyle modifications + 2-drug combination
- Preferably single-pill combination for adherence

Preferred Combinations:
- ACE inhibitor/ARB + CCB
- ACE inhibitor/ARB + thiazide diuretic
- CCB + thiazide diuretic

Compelling Indications:
- Heart failure: ACE inhibitor/ARB + beta-blocker + diuretic
- Post-MI: ACE inhibitor/ARB + beta-blocker
- Diabetes: ACE inhibitor/ARB + CCB or diuretic
- Chronic kidney disease: ACE inhibitor/ARB

RESISTANT HYPERTENSION

Definition: BP >130/80 mmHg despite 3 antihypertensive agents including a diuretic

Evaluation:
- Confirm true resistance (proper BP measurement, medication adherence)
- Screen for secondary causes
- Assess for drug interactions

Management:
- Optimize lifestyle modifications
- Ensure maximum tolerated doses
- Add spironolactone (most effective 4th agent)
- Consider other agents: amiloride, doxazosin, hydralazine
- Referral to hypertension specialist

Device-Based Therapies:
- Renal denervation (investigational)
- Carotid baroreceptor stimulation

HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCIES

Definition: Severe hypertension (>180/120 mmHg) with acute end-organ damage

Examples:
- Hypertensive encephalopathy
- Acute stroke
- Acute coronary syndrome
- Acute heart failure
- Acute kidney injury
- Aortic dissection
- Preeclampsia/eclampsia

Management:
- Immediate hospitalization
- IV antihypertensive agents
- Gradual BP reduction (10-20% in first hour)
- Avoid precipitous drops
- Identify and treat underlying cause

SPECIAL POPULATIONS

Elderly:
- Higher prevalence of isolated systolic hypertension
- Start low, go slow with medications
- Monitor for orthostatic hypotension
- Consider frailty and life expectancy

Pregnancy:
- Definition: BP ≥140/90 mmHg
- Preeclampsia: BP elevation + proteinuria or other features
- Safe medications: Methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine
- Avoid: ACE inhibitors, ARBs

Diabetes:
- Target <130/80 mmHg
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs preferred
- Multiple drug therapy often needed

Chronic Kidney Disease:
- Target <130/80 mmHg
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs slow progression
- Monitor electrolytes and creatinine

MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP

Initial Follow-up:
- 1-4 weeks after starting/changing therapy
- Monthly until BP controlled

Long-term Follow-up:
- Every 3-6 months once controlled
- Home BP monitoring encouraged
- Annual assessment of adherence and lifestyle

Laboratory Monitoring:
- Electrolytes and creatinine within 2-4 weeks of starting/changing ACE inhibitors/ARBs/diuretics
- Annual lipid profile
- Annual urinalysis and kidney function

PATIENT EDUCATION

Key Points:
1. Hypertension is usually asymptomatic
2. Lifelong condition requiring ongoing management
3. Importance of medication adherence
4. Lifestyle modifications remain important
5. Home BP monitoring technique
6. Signs and symptoms requiring immediate attention
7. Regular follow-up importance

Lifestyle Counseling:
- DASH diet principles
- Sodium reduction strategies
- Physical activity recommendations
- Weight management
- Stress reduction techniques
- Smoking cessation resources

PROGNOSIS

With Proper Treatment:
- Significant reduction in cardiovascular events
- 20-25% reduction in stroke risk
- 15-20% reduction in coronary events
- Slowing of kidney disease progression

Without Treatment:
- Progressive end-organ damage
- Increased cardiovascular mortality
- Accelerated atherosclerosis

The key to successful hypertension management is early detection, appropriate risk stratification, individualized treatment plans combining lifestyle modifications with evidence-based pharmacotherapy, and long-term patient engagement in care.
