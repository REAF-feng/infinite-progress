Acute Myocardial Infarction: Comprehensive Clinical Management Guide

DEFINITION
Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is the rapid development of myocardial necrosis caused by a critical imbalance between oxygen supply and demand. It represents a spectrum of acute coronary syndromes (ACS) and is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide.

CLASSIFICATION

Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (Fourth Definition):
Type 1: Spontaneous MI due to atherothrombotic plaque rupture/erosion
Type 2: MI secondary to oxygen supply-demand imbalance
Type 3: Sudden cardiac death with presumed MI
Type 4a: MI associated with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI)
Type 4b: MI associated with stent thrombosis
Type 5: MI associated with coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG)

STEMI vs NSTEMI Classification:
1. ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI):
   - Complete coronary artery occlusion
   - ST-segment elevation on ECG
   - Requires immediate reperfusion

2. Non-ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI):
   - Partial coronary artery occlusion
   - No ST-segment elevation
   - Managed based on risk stratification

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- Leading cause of death globally
- ~800,000 Americans experience MI annually
- ~605,000 are first-time attacks
- Incidence decreasing due to prevention efforts
- In-hospital mortality: STEMI ~7%, NSTEMI ~3-5%
- More common in men <75 years, women >75 years
- Racial disparities exist in outcomes

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

Atherosclerotic Plaque Development:
1. Endothelial dysfunction
2. Lipid accumulation in arterial wall
3. Inflammatory cell recruitment
4. Smooth muscle cell proliferation
5. Fibrous cap formation
6. Plaque vulnerability

Acute Thrombosis:
1. Plaque rupture or erosion
2. Exposure of thrombogenic material
3. Platelet activation and aggregation
4. Coagulation cascade activation
5. Thrombus formation
6. Coronary artery occlusion

Myocardial Necrosis Process:
- Begins within 20-40 minutes of complete occlusion
- Progresses from subendocardium to epicardium
- Irreversible after 6-8 hours
- Collateral circulation may provide protection

RISK FACTORS

Non-Modifiable:
- Age (men >45, women >55)
- Male gender (premenopausal women protected)
- Family history of CAD
- Genetic factors

Modifiable:
- Smoking (most important modifiable risk factor)
- Hypertension
- Diabetes mellitus
- Dyslipidemia
- Obesity (BMI >30)
- Physical inactivity
- Unhealthy diet
- Psychosocial stress

Emerging Risk Factors:
- C-reactive protein (CRP)
- Lipoprotein(a)
- Homocysteine
- Fibrinogen
- Sleep apnea
- Air pollution exposure
- Chronic kidney disease

CLINICAL PRESENTATION

Classic Symptoms:
1. Chest Pain:
   - Substernal, crushing, pressure-like
   - Radiation to left arm, neck, jaw, back
   - Duration >20 minutes
   - Not relieved by rest or nitroglycerin

2. Associated Symptoms:
   - Diaphoresis (sweating)
   - Nausea and vomiting
   - Shortness of breath
   - Lightheadedness
   - Feeling of impending doom

Atypical Presentations (30-40% of cases):
- Elderly patients (>75 years)
- Women
- Diabetics
- Patients with chronic kidney disease

Atypical Symptoms:
- Epigastric pain
- Indigestion-like symptoms
- Fatigue
- Weakness
- Syncope
- Isolated shortness of breath
- Back or shoulder pain

Silent MI:
- No symptoms or minimal symptoms
- More common in diabetics and elderly
- Diagnosed on routine ECG or imaging
- Up to 25% of MIs may be silent

Physical Examination:
May be normal or reveal:
- Diaphoresis
- Pallor
- Tachycardia or bradycardia
- Hypertension or hypotension
- S3 or S4 gallop
- Mitral regurgitation murmur
- Pulmonary rales
- Signs of cardiogenic shock

Killip Classification:
- Class I: No heart failure (mortality ~6%)
- Class II: Mild-moderate heart failure (mortality ~17%)
- Class III: Pulmonary edema (mortality ~38%)
- Class IV: Cardiogenic shock (mortality ~67%)

DIAGNOSTIC EVALUATION

Electrocardiogram (ECG):
Should be obtained within 10 minutes of presentation

STEMI Criteria:
- ST-elevation ≥1mm in 2 contiguous limb leads
- ST-elevation ≥2mm in 2 contiguous precordial leads
- New left bundle branch block

NSTEMI/Unstable Angina:
- ST-depression
- T-wave inversions
- Non-specific changes
- Normal ECG (doesn't exclude MI)

ECG Localization:
- Anterior: V1-V6 (LAD territory)
- Inferior: II, III, aVF (RCA or LCX territory)
- Lateral: I, aVL, V5-V6 (LCX territory)
- Posterior: Prominent R waves V1-V2

Cardiac Biomarkers:

Troponin (preferred):
- Troponin I or T
- Highly sensitive and specific
- Detects smaller amounts of myocardial necrosis
- Rises 3-6 hours post-MI
- Peaks 12-24 hours
- Remains elevated 7-14 days

High-Sensitivity Troponin:
- Earlier detection (1-3 hours)
- Better rule-out capability
- Lower false-positive rates
- Allows for rapid protocols

CK-MB:
- Less sensitive than troponin
- Useful for reinfarction detection
- Returns to normal sooner (3-4 days)

Other Biomarkers:
- Myoglobin (early marker, non-specific)
- LDH (late marker, non-specific)
- BNP/NT-proBNP (heart failure, prognosis)

Imaging:

Echocardiography:
- Assess wall motion abnormalities
- Evaluate left ventricular function
- Detect mechanical complications
- Portable and readily available

Chest X-ray:
- Assess for pulmonary edema
- Rule out other causes of chest pain
- Baseline for comparison

Coronary Angiography:
- Gold standard for coronary anatomy
- Therapeutic intervention capability
- Risk stratification
- Timing depends on presentation

MANAGEMENT

STEMI Management:
Primary goal: Rapid reperfusion (<90 minutes door-to-balloon)

Reperfusion Strategies:

1. Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):
   - Preferred strategy if available <90 minutes
   - Superior to fibrinolysis
   - Lower mortality and morbidity
   - Allows treatment of shock

2. Fibrinolytic Therapy:
   - When PCI not available or delayed >90 minutes
   - Most effective within 3 hours
   - Benefit up to 12 hours
   - Contraindications must be assessed

Fibrinolytic Agents:
- tPA (alteplase): 90-minute protocol
- TNK (tenecteplase): single bolus
- Streptokinase: less commonly used

Contraindications to Fibrinolysis:
Absolute:
- Active bleeding
- Intracranial hemorrhage history
- Known intracranial neoplasm
- Aortic dissection
- Significant closed head trauma <3 months

Relative:
- Age >75 years
- Current anticoagulation
- Pregnancy
- Recent surgery
- Severe hypertension (>180/110)

NSTEMI/Unstable Angina Management:

Risk Stratification:
TIMI Risk Score:
- Age ≥65
- ≥3 CAD risk factors
- Known CAD (stenosis ≥50%)
- Aspirin use in past 7 days
- Severe angina (≥2 episodes in 24h)
- ST changes ≥0.5mm
- Positive cardiac markers

GRACE Score:
- More comprehensive
- Includes vital signs and laboratory values
- Better predictor of outcomes

Management Strategy:
- High risk: Early invasive strategy (<24h)
- Intermediate risk: Invasive or conservative
- Low risk: Conservative management

Pharmacological Therapy:

Antiplatelet Agents:
1. Aspirin:
   - 325mg loading dose, then 81mg daily
   - Lifelong therapy
   - Chewed for faster absorption

2. P2Y12 Inhibitors:
   - Clopidogrel 600mg loading, then 75mg daily
   - Prasugrel 60mg loading, then 10mg daily
   - Ticagrelor 180mg loading, then 90mg BID
   - Duration: 12 months minimum

Anticoagulation:
- Unfractionated heparin
- Low molecular weight heparin (enoxaparin)
- Bivalirudin (direct thrombin inhibitor)
- Duration during hospitalization

Beta-Blockers:
- Metoprolol, carvedilol, bisoprolol
- Start within 24 hours if stable
- Reduce mortality and reinfarction
- Contraindicated in cardiogenic shock

ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:
- Start within 24 hours if stable
- Lisinopril, enalapril, losartan
- Reduce mortality and remodeling
- Monitor renal function and potassium

Statins:
- High-intensity statin therapy
- Atorvastatin 80mg or rosuvastatin 40mg
- Start before discharge
- LDL goal <70 mg/dL

COMPLICATIONS

Mechanical Complications (1-3% of STEMIs):
1. Ventricular Septal Defect:
   - Usually 3-7 days post-MI
   - New holosystolic murmur
   - Requires urgent surgical repair

2. Papillary Muscle Rupture:
   - Acute severe mitral regurgitation
   - Pulmonary edema
   - Cardiogenic shock

3. Free Wall Rupture:
   - Cardiac tamponade
   - High mortality
   - Emergency pericardiocentesis/surgery

Electrical Complications:
1. Arrhythmias:
   - Ventricular tachycardia/fibrillation
   - Complete heart block (inferior MI)
   - Atrial fibrillation

2. Sudden Cardiac Death:
   - Primary electrical event
   - Most common in first hour

Hemodynamic Complications:
1. Cardiogenic Shock:
   - 5-8% of STEMIs
   - Mortality 50-80%
   - Requires immediate revascularization

2. Heart Failure:
   - Acute or chronic
   - Related to infarct size
   - May require device therapy

Other Complications:
1. Pericarditis:
   - 1-3 days post-MI
   - Pleuritic chest pain
   - Pericardial friction rub

2. Dressler Syndrome:
   - Weeks to months post-MI
   - Autoimmune pericarditis
   - Fever, pleuritic pain

3. Left Ventricular Aneurysm:
   - Late complication
   - Risk of thromboembolism
   - May require surgical repair

SECONDARY PREVENTION

Lifestyle Modifications:
1. Smoking Cessation:
   - Most important intervention
   - Reduces risk by 50%
   - Nicotine replacement therapy

2. Diet:
   - Mediterranean diet
   - Low saturated fat
   - Omega-3 fatty acids
   - Limit sodium

3. Exercise:
   - Cardiac rehabilitation
   - 150 minutes moderate activity/week
   - Supervised program preferred

4. Weight Management:
   - BMI 18.5-24.9 kg/m²
   - Waist circumference goals

Pharmacological Therapy:
1. Dual Antiplatelet Therapy:
   - Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor
   - Minimum 12 months
   - Longer in high-risk patients

2. Beta-Blockers:
   - Lifelong therapy
   - Reduce sudden death
   - Target heart rate 50-60 bpm

3. ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:
   - Especially if EF <40%
   - Remodeling prevention
   - Blood pressure control

4. Statins:
   - High-intensity therapy
   - LDL <70 mg/dL
   - Consider PCSK9 inhibitors if needed

5. Additional Therapies:
   - Eplerenone (if EF <35%)
   - Diabetes management
   - Blood pressure control

Device Therapy:
1. Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator (ICD):
   - EF ≤35% after 3 months
   - Primary prevention sudden death
   - Life vest as bridge

2. Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy:
   - EF ≤35% with wide QRS
   - Improves symptoms and survival

PROGNOSIS

Factors Affecting Prognosis:
- Age at presentation
- Infarct size and location
- Left ventricular function
- Time to reperfusion
- Presence of complications
- Comorbidities

Mortality:
- 30-day mortality: STEMI ~7%, NSTEMI ~3-5%
- 1-year mortality: ~10%
- Sudden death risk highest in first 30 days

Quality of Life:
- Most patients return to normal activities
- Cardiac rehabilitation improves outcomes
- Depression common (20-25%)
- Return to work in 2-8 weeks

Long-term Management:
- Annual cardiology follow-up
- Medication adherence crucial
- Risk factor modification
- Surveillance for complications
- Family screening for CAD risk factors

The management of acute myocardial infarction requires rapid recognition, immediate reperfusion therapy, comprehensive medical management, and lifelong secondary prevention strategies. Multidisciplinary care and patient education are essential for optimal outcomes.
