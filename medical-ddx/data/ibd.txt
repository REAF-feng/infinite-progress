Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

Overview:
IBD comprises two main conditions: Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. These are chronic inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract characterized by periods of relapse and remission.

Crohn's Disease:
Characteristics:
- Can affect any part of GI tract (mouth to anus)
- Transmural inflammation (all layers of bowel wall)
- Skip lesions (discontinuous involvement)
- Granulomatous inflammation
- Higher risk of strictures and fistulas

Ulcerative Colitis:
Characteristics:
- Limited to colon and rectum
- Mucosal and submucosal inflammation only
- Continuous involvement starting from rectum
- Crypt abscesses and pseudopolyps
- Higher risk of dysplasia and cancer

Pathophysiology:
- Inappropriate immune response to intestinal microbiota
- Genetic susceptibility (>200 risk loci identified)
- Environmental triggers
- Dysregulated cytokine production
- Compromised intestinal barrier function
- Altered gut microbiome

Clinical Presentation:
Common Symptoms:
- Abdominal pain and cramping
- Diarrhea (bloody in UC, may be bloody in CD)
- Weight loss and malnutrition
- Fatigue
- Fever during flares
- Reduced appetite

Crohn's Disease Specific:
- Right lower quadrant pain (ileocecal involvement)
- Perianal disease (fistulas, abscesses)
- Oral ulcers
- Growth retardation in children
- Malabsorption symptoms

Ulcerative Colitis Specific:
- Left lower quadrant pain
- Bloody diarrhea
- Urgency and tenesmus
- Rectal bleeding
- Mucus in stool

Extraintestinal Manifestations:
Musculoskeletal:
- Arthritis (peripheral and axial)
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Sacroiliitis

Dermatologic:
- Erythema nodosum
- Pyoderma gangrenosum
- Aphthous ulcers

Ocular:
- Episcleritis
- Uveitis
- Iritis

Hepatobiliary:
- Primary sclerosing cholangitis (especially UC)
- Fatty liver
- Gallstones

Risk Factors:
Genetic:
- Family history (10-15% have affected relative)
- Ashkenazi Jewish descent
- Multiple susceptibility genes

Environmental:
- Smoking (increases CD risk, protective for UC)
- Westernized diet
- Antibiotic use
- Stress
- Infection triggers

Demographics:
- Bimodal age distribution (15-35, 55-65 years)
- Slightly higher in females
- More common in developed countries

Diagnosis:
Clinical Assessment:
- Detailed history and physical examination
- Family history
- Symptom duration and pattern
- Growth assessment in children

Laboratory Tests:
Inflammatory Markers:
- C-reactive protein (CRP)
- Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)
- Fecal calprotectin or lactoferrin

Blood Tests:
- Complete blood count (anemia, thrombocytosis)
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- Albumin and nutritional markers
- Iron studies, B12, folate

Stool Studies:
- Fecal calprotectin (>150 μg/g suggests IBD)
- C. difficile toxin
- Stool culture and parasites
- Fecal lactoferrin

Serologic Markers:
- ASCA (anti-Saccharomyces cerevisiae antibodies) - Crohn's
- p-ANCA (perinuclear anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies) - UC
- Limited diagnostic utility

Imaging:
CT Enterography:
- Preferred for small bowel evaluation
- Bowel wall thickening
- Strictures and fistulas
- Abscesses

MR Enterography:
- No radiation exposure
- Excellent soft tissue contrast
- Preferred in young patients
- Assessment of perianal disease

Small Bowel Series:
- Less commonly used
- May show strictures and ulcerations

Ultrasound:
- Point-of-care assessment
- Bowel wall thickness
- Disease activity monitoring

Endoscopy:
Colonoscopy:
- Gold standard for diagnosis
- Assess extent and severity
- Obtain biopsies
- Monitor for dysplasia

Upper Endoscopy:
- If upper GI symptoms
- Evaluate for Crohn's disease
- Rule out other conditions

Capsule Endoscopy:
- Small bowel evaluation
- Contraindicated if stricture suspected
- High sensitivity for mucosal lesions

Balloon Enteroscopy:
- Deep small bowel examination
- Therapeutic interventions
- Limited availability

Histopathology:
Crohn's Disease:
- Transmural inflammation
- Non-caseating granulomas (40% of cases)
- Lymphoid aggregates
- Cryptitis and crypt abscesses

Ulcerative Colitis:
- Mucosal inflammation
- Crypt architectural distortion
- Increased chronic inflammatory cells
- Crypt abscesses

Treatment:
Medical Therapy:
Aminosalicylates (5-ASA):
- Mesalamine, sulfasalazine, balsalazide
- First-line for mild-moderate UC
- Topical formulations for distal disease
- Limited efficacy in Crohn's disease

Corticosteroids:
- Prednisone, budesonide
- Induction therapy for moderate-severe disease
- Not for maintenance therapy
- Taper once remission achieved

Immunomodulators:
Thiopurines:
- Azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine
- Steroid-sparing agents
- Maintenance therapy
- TPMT testing before initiation

Methotrexate:
- Alternative to thiopurines
- Especially in Crohn's disease
- Weekly dosing with folic acid

Biologic Therapy:
Anti-TNF Agents:
- Infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab, golimumab
- Moderate-severe disease
- Steroid-refractory cases
- Fistulizing Crohn's disease

Anti-Integrin Agents:
- Vedolizumab
- Gut-selective mechanism
- Lower systemic side effects
- Effective for both CD and UC

Anti-IL12/23 Agents:
- Ustekinumab
- Effective for Crohn's disease
- Alternative mechanism of action

JAK Inhibitors:
- Tofacitinib (UC)
- Oral administration
- Rapid onset of action

Small Molecule Therapy:
- Ozanimod (UC) - S1P receptor modulator
- Oral administration
- Cardiac monitoring required

Combination Therapy:
- Anti-TNF + immunomodulator
- More effective than monotherapy
- Higher immunosuppression risk
- Consider in severe disease

Surgical Treatment:
Crohn's Disease:
Indications:
- Bowel obstruction
- Perforation
- Abscesses
- Medically refractory disease
- Growth failure in children

Procedures:
- Stricturoplasty
- Bowel resection
- Drainage of abscesses
- Fistula repair

Ulcerative Colitis:
Indications:
- Medically refractory disease
- Dysplasia or cancer
- Severe complications
- Growth failure

Procedures:
- Total proctocolectomy with ileostomy
- Ileal pouch-anal anastomosis (IPAA)
- Subtotal colectomy

Monitoring:
Disease Activity Assessment:
- Clinical symptoms
- Laboratory markers (CRP, fecal calprotectin)
- Endoscopic evaluation
- Cross-sectional imaging

Treat-to-Target Approach:
- Clinical remission
- Endoscopic healing
- Histologic remission (emerging target)
- Biomarker normalization

Surveillance:
Cancer Screening:
- Colonoscopy every 1-3 years after 8-10 years of disease
- Chromoendoscopy or narrow-band imaging
- Random biopsies throughout colon

Complications:
Intestinal:
- Strictures and obstruction
- Perforation
- Fistulas and abscesses
- Malabsorption
- Colorectal cancer

Medication-Related:
- Infections (opportunistic)
- Malignancy (lymphoma, skin cancer)
- Bone disease
- Liver toxicity

Growth and Development:
- Growth retardation in children
- Delayed puberty
- Nutritional deficiencies

Prognosis:
Crohn's Disease:
- Chronic relapsing-remitting course
- 80% require surgery within 20 years
- Strictures and fistulas common
- Anti-TNF therapy improves outcomes

Ulcerative Colitis:
- 10% require surgery within 10 years
- Risk of colorectal cancer
- Colectomy is curative
- Generally better prognosis than CD

Quality of Life:
- Significantly impacted during flares
- Improved with effective treatment
- Psychological support important
- Patient education essential

Special Populations:
Pregnancy:
- Most medications safe during pregnancy
- Disease activity more harmful than medications
- Sulfasalazine requires folate supplementation
- Biologic therapy generally continued

Pediatric IBD:
- More extensive disease at presentation
- Growth and development concerns
- Nutritional therapy important
- Family and school support needed

Key Points:
- Chronic inflammatory conditions requiring lifelong management
- Early diagnosis and treatment improve outcomes
- Treat-to-target approach with objective measures
- Multidisciplinary care team approach
- Regular monitoring for complications
- Patient education and support essential
- Surgery may be necessary despite medical therapy
- Quality of life can be significantly improved with proper treatment
