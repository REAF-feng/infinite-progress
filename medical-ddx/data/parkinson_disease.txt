Parkinson's Disease

Overview:
Parkinson's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder affecting movement, characterized by tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, and postural instability. It results from the loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra.

Pathophysiology:
- Degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta
- Formation of Lewy bodies containing alpha-synuclein protein
- Disruption of dopamine pathways in basal ganglia
- Progressive loss of motor control
- Non-motor symptoms due to widespread neurodegeneration

Clinical Presentation:
Motor Symptoms (Cardinal Features):
- Resting tremor (4-6 Hz, "pill-rolling")
- Rigidity (cogwheel or lead-pipe)
- Bradykinesia (slowness of movement)
- Postural instability (later stage)

Early Motor Signs:
- Reduced arm swing while walking
- Micrographia (small handwriting)
- Hypophonia (soft speech)
- Masked facies (reduced facial expression)
- Shuffling gait

Non-Motor Symptoms:
Neuropsychiatric:
- Depression and anxiety
- Apathy
- Cognitive impairment
- Hallucinations (visual)
- Sleep disorders (REM sleep behavior disorder)

Autonomic:
- Constipation
- Orthostatic hypotension
- Urinary dysfunction
- Sexual dysfunction
- Excessive sweating

Sensory:
- Loss of smell (anosmia)
- Pain and sensory symptoms
- Restless leg syndrome

Sleep:
- Insomnia
- Excessive daytime sleepiness
- REM sleep behavior disorder
- Sleep fragmentation

Risk Factors:
- Age (most important risk factor)
- Male gender
- Family history/genetic factors
- Environmental toxins (pesticides, heavy metals)
- Head trauma
- Rural living
- Well water consumption

Protective Factors:
- Caffeine consumption
- Smoking (paradoxical)
- Uric acid levels
- Physical exercise

Diagnosis:
Clinical Criteria:
- Presence of bradykinesia plus at least one of:
  * Resting tremor
  * Rigidity
  * Response to dopaminergic therapy

Supportive Features:
- Unilateral onset
- Progressive disorder
- Persistent asymmetry
- Excellent response to levodopa
- Levodopa-induced dyskinesia
- Clinical course >10 years

Imaging:
- DaTscan (dopamine transporter SPECT) - may help in uncertain cases
- MRI to exclude other causes
- No routine imaging required for typical cases

Laboratory Tests:
- No specific tests for Parkinson's disease
- Tests to exclude other conditions

Differential Diagnosis:
- Essential tremor
- Drug-induced parkinsonism
- Vascular parkinsonism
- Progressive supranuclear palsy
- Multiple system atrophy
- Corticobasal degeneration
- Normal pressure hydrocephalus
- Wilson's disease

Treatment:
Pharmacological:
Dopamine Replacement:
- Levodopa/carbidopa (gold standard)
- Immediate-release and controlled-release formulations
- Monitor for motor complications

Dopamine Agonists:
- Pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine
- May delay motor complications
- Risk of impulse control disorders

MAO-B Inhibitors:
- Selegiline, rasagiline, safinamide
- Mild symptomatic benefit
- May have neuroprotective effects

COMT Inhibitors:
- Entacapone, tolcapone
- Used with levodopa to extend duration
- Reduce wearing-off symptoms

Anticholinergics:
- Benztropine, trihexyphenidyl
- Mainly for tremor in younger patients
- Avoid in elderly due to cognitive effects

Amantadine:
- For dyskinesia
- Mild anti-parkinsonian effects

Surgical Treatment:
Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS):
- Subthalamic nucleus or globus pallidus
- For patients with motor complications
- Reduces medication requirements
- Improves quality of life

Non-Pharmacological Treatment:
Physical Therapy:
- Gait training
- Balance exercises
- Flexibility and strength training
- LSVT BIG program

Speech Therapy:
- LSVT LOUD program
- Voice amplification
- Swallowing assessment

Occupational Therapy:
- Activities of daily living
- Home safety assessment
- Adaptive equipment

Exercise:
- Regular aerobic exercise
- Dancing, tai chi, yoga
- May be neuroprotective

Complications:
Motor Complications:
- Wearing-off phenomenon
- On-off fluctuations
- Dyskinesia
- Freezing episodes

Non-Motor Complications:
- Dementia (30-40% develop)
- Depression
- Psychosis
- Autonomic dysfunction
- Sleep disorders

Prognosis:
- Variable progression rate
- Life expectancy may be reduced
- Quality of life significantly affected
- Motor complications develop in most patients
- Non-motor symptoms become prominent in later stages

Management Strategies:
Early Disease:
- Exercise and lifestyle modifications
- Consider dopamine agonist or MAO-B inhibitor
- Delay levodopa if possible

Moderate Disease:
- Levodopa/carbidopa
- Add adjunctive medications as needed
- Monitor for complications

Advanced Disease:
- Optimize medication regimens
- Consider DBS
- Manage non-motor symptoms
- Palliative care approach

Key Points:
- Clinical diagnosis based on motor features
- Excellent response to levodopa is diagnostic hallmark
- Early treatment focuses on symptom control
- Motor complications are inevitable with disease progression
- Non-motor symptoms significantly impact quality of life
- Multidisciplinary approach essential
- Exercise may be disease-modifying
