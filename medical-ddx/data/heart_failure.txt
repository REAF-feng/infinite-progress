Heart Failure

Overview:
Heart failure is a complex clinical syndrome characterized by the heart's inability to pump blood efficiently to meet the body's metabolic demands. It can result from various structural or functional cardiac disorders.

Pathophysiology:
- Reduced cardiac output and/or elevated filling pressures
- Activation of neurohormonal systems (RAAS, SNS)
- Ventricular remodeling and dysfunction
- Fluid retention and congestion
- Progressive deterioration of cardiac function

Classification:
By Ejection Fraction:
Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF):
- LVEF ≤40%
- Systolic dysfunction
- Most common in younger patients
- Better evidence for therapies

Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF):
- LVEF ≥50%
- Diastolic dysfunction
- More common in elderly, women
- Limited treatment options

Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFmrEF):
- LVEF 41-49%
- Intermediate category
- May respond to HFrEF therapies

By Functional Class (NYHA):
Class I: No symptoms with ordinary activity
Class II: Symptoms with ordinary activity
Class III: Symptoms with minimal activity
Class IV: Symptoms at rest

Clinical Presentation:
Left Heart Failure:
- Dyspnea on exertion
- Orthopnea
- Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea
- Fatigue and weakness
- Reduced exercise tolerance
- Cough (often nocturnal)

Right Heart Failure:
- Peripheral edema
- Jugular venous distension
- Ascites
- Right upper quadrant pain
- Early satiety and bloating
- Weight gain

Acute Decompensation:
- Sudden worsening of symptoms
- Pulmonary edema
- Cardiogenic shock
- Acute kidney injury

Physical Examination:
Vital Signs:
- Tachycardia
- Elevated blood pressure (may be low in advanced disease)
- Tachypnea
- Reduced oxygen saturation

Cardiovascular:
- S3 gallop (ventricular gallop)
- S4 gallop (atrial gallop)
- Murmurs (mitral regurgitation, tricuspid regurgitation)
- Displaced point of maximal impulse

Pulmonary:
- Rales/crackles
- Pleural effusions
- Reduced breath sounds

Other:
- Jugular venous distension
- Peripheral edema
- Hepatomegaly
- Ascites
- Cool extremities

Etiology:
Ischemic Heart Disease:
- Coronary artery disease
- Previous myocardial infarction
- Chronic ischemia

Cardiomyopathies:
- Dilated cardiomyopathy
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
- Restrictive cardiomyopathy
- Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy

Valvular Disease:
- Mitral regurgitation/stenosis
- Aortic regurgitation/stenosis
- Tricuspid regurgitation
- Pulmonary valve disease

Hypertensive Heart Disease:
- Long-standing hypertension
- Left ventricular hypertrophy
- Diastolic dysfunction

Other Causes:
- Viral myocarditis
- Alcohol-induced cardiomyopathy
- Chemotherapy-induced cardiomyopathy
- Thyroid disease
- Infiltrative diseases (amyloidosis, sarcoidosis)

Diagnosis:
Laboratory Tests:
- B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) or NT-proBNP
- Complete blood count
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- Liver function tests
- Thyroid function tests
- Urinalysis

Electrocardiogram:
- Arrhythmias
- Evidence of prior MI
- Left ventricular hypertrophy
- Conduction abnormalities

Chest X-ray:
- Cardiomegaly
- Pulmonary edema
- Pleural effusions
- Pulmonary vascular congestion

Echocardiography:
- Left ventricular ejection fraction
- Wall motion abnormalities
- Valvular function
- Diastolic function assessment
- Right heart function

Additional Testing:
- Cardiac catheterization (if ischemic etiology suspected)
- Cardiac MRI (for tissue characterization)
- Nuclear imaging
- Exercise testing
- Right heart catheterization (in selected patients)

Treatment:
HFrEF Management:
ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:
- First-line therapy
- Reduce mortality and morbidity
- Start low dose, titrate to maximum tolerated

Beta-blockers:
- Metoprolol succinate, carvedilol, bisoprolol
- Reduce mortality
- Start low, titrate slowly

Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists:
- Spironolactone, eplerenone
- For NYHA Class II-IV symptoms
- Monitor potassium and kidney function

SGLT2 Inhibitors:
- Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin
- Reduce heart failure hospitalizations
- Benefits regardless of diabetes status

ARNI (Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor):
- Sacubitril/valsartan
- Superior to ACE inhibitors in reducing cardiovascular death
- For patients already on ACE inhibitor/ARB

Diuretics:
- Loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide)
- For volume overload
- Monitor electrolytes and kidney function

Device Therapy:
Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD):
- Primary prevention: LVEF ≤35% on optimal medical therapy
- Secondary prevention: survivors of VT/VF

Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy (CRT):
- LVEF ≤35%, QRS ≥120ms, NYHA Class II-IV
- Improves symptoms and mortality

HFpEF Management:
- Limited evidence-based therapies
- Treatment of comorbidities
- Diuretics for congestion
- SGLT2 inhibitors may be beneficial
- Blood pressure control
- Rate control for atrial fibrillation

Acute Heart Failure:
Initial Assessment:
- Hemodynamic profile (warm/cold, wet/dry)
- Volume status
- Blood pressure
- Kidney function

Treatment:
- IV diuretics
- Vasodilators (nitroglycerin, hydralazine)
- Inotropes (dobutamine, milrinone) - if hypotensive
- Mechanical circulatory support (severe cases)

Lifestyle Modifications:
- Sodium restriction (<2-3g/day)
- Fluid restriction (if hyponatremic)
- Daily weight monitoring
- Regular exercise (cardiac rehabilitation)
- Smoking cessation
- Alcohol limitation
- Medication adherence

Monitoring:
- Regular clinical assessment
- Laboratory monitoring (BNP, electrolytes, kidney function)
- Echocardiography
- Exercise testing
- Quality of life assessments

Complications:
- Arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, ventricular arrhythmias)
- Sudden cardiac death
- Thromboembolism
- Progressive kidney disease
- Liver dysfunction
- Depression and cognitive impairment

Prognosis:
- 5-year mortality rate 30-50%
- Frequent hospitalizations
- Progressive functional decline
- Better outcomes with HFrEF than HFpEF
- Early diagnosis and treatment improve outcomes

Advanced Therapies:
- Heart transplantation
- Left ventricular assist devices (LVADs)
- Total artificial heart
- Experimental therapies (gene therapy, stem cells)

Key Points:
- Common and growing public health problem
- Classification by ejection fraction guides treatment
- Neurohormonal blockade is cornerstone of HFrEF therapy
- Device therapy indicated for selected patients
- Early recognition and treatment improve outcomes
- Multidisciplinary care approach
- Patient education and self-care essential
