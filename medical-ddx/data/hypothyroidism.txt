Hypothyroidism

Overview:
Hypothyroidism is a common endocrine disorder characterized by insufficient production of thyroid hormones, leading to decreased metabolic rate and various systemic effects.

Pathophysiology:
- Decreased T4 and T3 production
- Elevated TSH (primary hypothyroidism)
- Reduced metabolic rate
- Protein synthesis alterations
- Cardiovascular effects
- Neurological dysfunction

Types:
Primary Hypothyroidism:
- Thyroid gland dysfunction
- Elevated TSH, low T4
- Most common type (95%)

Secondary Hypothyroidism:
- Pituitary dysfunction
- Low TSH and T4
- Rare (5% of cases)

Tertiary Hypothyroidism:
- Hypothalamic dysfunction
- Very rare

Clinical Presentation:
Common Symptoms:
- Fatigue and weakness
- Weight gain
- Cold intolerance
- Constipation
- Depression
- Memory problems
- Dry skin and hair
- Hair loss

Physical Signs:
- Bradycardia
- Diastolic hypertension
- Cool, dry, rough skin
- Brittle nails
- Hoarse voice
- Delayed reflexes
- Myxedema

Etiology:
Hashimoto's Thyroiditis:
- Most common cause in iodine-sufficient areas
- Autoimmune destruction
- Anti-TPO and anti-thyroglobulin antibodies

Iodine Deficiency:
- Most common cause worldwide
- Endemic in certain regions
- Preventable with iodine supplementation

Post-surgical:
- Total or partial thyroidectomy
- Immediate or delayed onset

Radioactive Iodine:
- Treatment for hyperthyroidism
- Gradual onset over months to years

Medications:
- Lithium
- Amiodarone
- Interferon
- Tyrosine kinase inhibitors

Congenital:
- Thyroid dysgenesis
- Inborn errors of hormone synthesis

Diagnosis:
Laboratory Tests:
TSH:
- First-line screening test
- Elevated in primary hypothyroidism
- Normal: 0.4-4.0 mIU/L

Free T4:
- Confirms diagnosis
- Low in overt hypothyroidism
- Normal in subclinical hypothyroidism

Free T3:
- Usually not necessary for diagnosis
- May be normal in mild hypothyroidism

Thyroid Antibodies:
- Anti-TPO antibodies
- Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies
- Helpful in determining etiology

Treatment:
Levothyroxine:
- Synthetic T4
- Standard treatment
- Once daily dosing
- Start low, titrate slowly

Dosing:
- Initial: 1.6 μg/kg/day (healthy adults)
- Elderly: 25-50 μg/day
- Cardiac disease: 25 μg/day
- Adjust every 6-8 weeks

Monitoring:
- TSH every 6-8 weeks until stable
- Annual monitoring once stable
- Adjust dose to normalize TSH

Special Considerations:
- Take on empty stomach
- Avoid calcium, iron, coffee
- Drug interactions
- Pregnancy requirements higher

Subclinical Hypothyroidism:
- Elevated TSH, normal T4
- Often asymptomatic
- Treatment controversial
- Consider if TSH >10 or symptoms

Myxedema Coma:
- Medical emergency
- Altered mental status
- Hypothermia
- IV levothyroxine
- Supportive care

Pregnancy:
- Increased levothyroxine needs
- Monitor TSH each trimester
- Target TSH <2.5 first trimester
- Fetal development important

Key Points:
- Common endocrine disorder
- TSH is primary screening test
- Levothyroxine is standard treatment
- Monitor and adjust dosing regularly
- Special considerations in pregnancy
