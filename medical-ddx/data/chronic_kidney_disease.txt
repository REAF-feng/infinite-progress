Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Overview:
Chronic kidney disease is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for >3 months, with implications for health. It represents a global health challenge with increasing prevalence.

Classification (KDIGO):
Stage 1: GFR ≥90 mL/min/1.73m² + kidney damage
Stage 2: GFR 60-89 mL/min/1.73m² + kidney damage
Stage 3a: GFR 45-59 mL/min/1.73m²
Stage 3b: GFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m²
Stage 4: GFR 15-29 mL/min/1.73m²
Stage 5: GFR <15 mL/min/1.73m² or on dialysis

Etiology:
- Diabetes mellitus (leading cause)
- Hypertension
- Glomerulonephritis
- Polycystic kidney disease
- Autoimmune diseases
- Hereditary nephritis

Clinical Presentation:
Early stages often asymptomatic
Advanced stages:
- Fatigue and weakness
- Uremic symptoms
- Fluid retention
- Bone disease
- Anemia
- Cardiovascular complications

Diagnosis:
- Serum creatinine and eGFR
- Urinalysis and proteinuria
- Imaging studies
- Kidney biopsy (selected cases)

Treatment:
- Blood pressure control
- Diabetes management
- Proteinuria reduction
- Cardiovascular risk modification
- Mineral bone disease management
- Anemia treatment
- Preparation for renal replacement therapy
