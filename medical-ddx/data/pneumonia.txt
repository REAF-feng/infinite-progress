Pneumonia: Comprehensive Clinical Management Guide

DEFINITION
Pneumonia is an acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma characterized by inflammation of the alveoli and surrounding lung tissue. It results in impaired gas exchange and can range from mild illness to life-threatening disease requiring intensive care.

CLASSIFICATION

By Setting of Acquisition:
1. Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):
   - Occurs in outpatient setting or within 48 hours of hospital admission
   - Most common type
   - Different microbiology than healthcare-associated

2. Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP):
   - Develops ≥48 hours after hospital admission
   - Excludes ventilator-associated pneumonia
   - Higher risk of multidrug-resistant organisms

3. Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP):
   - Develops ≥48 hours after endotracheal intubation
   - High morbidity and mortality
   - Often caused by resistant organisms

4. Healthcare-Associated Pneumonia (HCAP):
   - Concept has evolved
   - Patients with recent healthcare exposure
   - Risk factors for resistant organisms

By Pathogen Type:
- Bacterial pneumonia (most common)
- Viral pneumonia
- Fungal pneumonia
- Atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella)
- Pneumocystis pneumonia (immunocompromised)

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- 4-5 million cases annually in the US
- Leading infectious cause of death worldwide
- 8th leading cause of death in the US
- Hospitalization rate: 20-25% of patients
- ICU admission: 5-10% of hospitalized patients
- Higher incidence in winter months
- Bimodal age distribution: <2 years and >65 years

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Normal lung defenses include:
- Upper respiratory tract filtration
- Mucociliary escalator
- Alveolar macrophages
- Surfactant
- Immunoglobulins (IgA, IgG)
- Complement system

Pneumonia develops when:
1. Pathogen load overwhelms defenses
2. Highly virulent organism
3. Impaired host defenses
4. Aspiration of infected material

Routes of Infection:
- Aspiration (most common)
- Inhalation of aerosolized particles
- Hematogenous spread
- Direct extension from contiguous infection

Inflammatory Response:
- Neutrophil recruitment
- Increased vascular permeability
- Alveolar filling with inflammatory exudate
- Impaired gas exchange
- V/Q mismatch

COMMON PATHOGENS

Community-Acquired Pneumonia:
1. Streptococcus pneumoniae (most common)
   - Gram-positive diplococci
   - Encapsulated organism
   - Higher risk in immunocompromised

2. Haemophilus influenzae
   - Gram-negative coccobacillus
   - Associated with COPD, smoking

3. Mycoplasma pneumoniae
   - Atypical pneumonia
   - Young adults, outbreaks
   - "Walking pneumonia"

4. Chlamydia pneumoniae
   - Atypical pneumonia
   - Chronic respiratory symptoms

5. Legionella pneumophila
   - Water-associated transmission
   - Severe pneumonia, systemic symptoms
   - Risk factors: immunosuppression, advanced age

6. Viral pathogens:
   - Influenza A/B
   - Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)
   - Parainfluenza virus
   - Adenovirus
   - SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19)

Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia:
1. Staphylococcus aureus
   - Including MRSA
   - High mortality

2. Pseudomonas aeruginosa
   - Multidrug resistance common
   - Higher risk in immunocompromised

3. Klebsiella pneumoniae
   - ESBL-producing strains
   - Necrotizing pneumonia

4. Acinetobacter baumannii
   - Multidrug resistance
   - ICU-associated

5. Enterobacter species

CLINICAL PRESENTATION

Classic Symptoms:
- Fever (may be absent in elderly/immunocompromised)
- Productive cough (purulent sputum)
- Dyspnea
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Chills and rigors

Atypical Presentation:
- Gradual onset
- Dry cough
- Headache
- Myalgia
- Fatigue
- Low-grade fever

Elderly/Immunocompromised:
- Altered mental status
- Weakness
- Delirium
- Falls
- Minimal respiratory symptoms

Physical Examination:
- Fever (temperature >38°C or <36°C)
- Tachypnea (respiratory rate >20/min)
- Tachycardia
- Hypotension (severe cases)
- Oxygen desaturation

Pulmonary Findings:
- Dullness to percussion
- Increased tactile fremitus
- Bronchial breath sounds
- Crackles (rales)
- Pleural friction rub
- Diminished breath sounds (pleural effusion)

Extrapulmonary Signs:
- Cyanosis
- Use of accessory muscles
- Confusion/altered mental status
- Signs of sepsis

DIAGNOSTIC EVALUATION

Laboratory Tests:
1. Complete Blood Count (CBC):
   - Leukocytosis (>12,000/μL) or leukopenia (<4,000/μL)
   - Left shift (increased neutrophils)
   - May be normal in atypical pneumonia

2. Basic Metabolic Panel:
   - Electrolyte abnormalities
   - Acute kidney injury
   - Acid-base status

3. Inflammatory markers:
   - C-reactive protein (CRP)
   - Procalcitonin (bacterial vs viral)
   - Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)

4. Arterial Blood Gas (if indicated):
   - Hypoxemia
   - Acid-base disorders
   - Alveolar-arterial gradient

Microbiological Testing:

Outpatient CAP:
- Generally not recommended
- Consider if failed outpatient therapy

Inpatient CAP:
- Blood cultures (before antibiotics)
- Sputum culture (if good quality specimen)
- Urinary antigens:
  * S. pneumoniae
  * Legionella pneumophila serogroup 1

Severe CAP/ICU:
- Blood cultures
- Sputum culture
- Urinary antigens
- Consider bronchoscopy with BAL
- Pleural fluid analysis (if effusion present)

Additional Testing:
- Respiratory viral panel (especially during flu season)
- Mycoplasma/Chlamydia testing
- Fungal studies (immunocompromised)
- Tuberculosis testing (risk factors present)

Imaging:

Chest Radiograph:
- First-line imaging
- Confirms diagnosis
- Assesses extent and complications
- Patterns:
  * Lobar consolidation (typical bacterial)
  * Interstitial infiltrates (atypical/viral)
  * Bilateral infiltrates (severe disease)

CT Chest:
- Not routinely indicated
- Consider if:
  * Complications suspected
  * Poor response to treatment
  * Immunocompromised patient
  * Suspected malignancy

SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

CURB-65 Score (Community-Acquired Pneumonia):
- Confusion
- Urea >19 mg/dL (7 mmol/L)
- Respiratory rate ≥30/min
- Blood pressure <90/60 mmHg
- Age ≥65 years

Score interpretation:
- 0-1: Outpatient treatment
- 2: Inpatient treatment
- ≥3: Consider ICU admission

PSI (Pneumonia Severity Index):
- More complex scoring system
- Includes age, comorbidities, physical findings, laboratory values
- Classes I-V
- Classes I-III: outpatient
- Classes IV-V: inpatient

SMART-COP Score:
- Predicts need for intensive respiratory support
- Systolic BP <90 mmHg
- Multilobar involvement
- Albumin <3.5 g/dL
- Respiratory rate (age-adjusted)
- Tachycardia >125 bpm
- Confusion
- Oxygenation (age-adjusted)
- pH <7.35

ICU Admission Criteria:
Major criteria (1 required):
- Invasive mechanical ventilation
- Septic shock requiring vasopressors

Minor criteria (3 required):
- Respiratory rate ≥30/min
- PaO2/FiO2 ratio ≤250
- Multilobar infiltrates
- Confusion/disorientation
- Uremia (BUN ≥20 mg/dL)
- Leukopenia (WBC <4,000/μL)
- Thrombocytopenia (platelets <100,000/μL)
- Hypothermia (core temperature <36°C)
- Hypotension requiring aggressive fluid resuscitation

TREATMENT

Antibiotic Therapy:

Outpatient CAP:
Healthy adults, no risk factors:
- Amoxicillin 1g TID or
- Doxycycline 100mg BID or
- Macrolide (azithromycin 500mg daily × 3 days)

Comorbidities or antibiotic use in past 90 days:
- Amoxicillin/clavulanate 875/125mg BID PLUS macrolide or
- Fluoroquinolone (levofloxacin 750mg daily)

Inpatient CAP (non-ICU):
- Beta-lactam PLUS macrolide:
  * Ampicillin 1-2g q6h + azithromycin 500mg daily
  * Ceftriaxone 1g daily + azithromycin 500mg daily
- Or fluoroquinolone monotherapy:
  * Levofloxacin 750mg daily
  * Moxifloxacin 400mg daily

Inpatient CAP (ICU):
- Beta-lactam PLUS macrolide:
  * Ceftriaxone 1g daily + azithromycin 500mg daily
  * Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g q6h + azithromycin
- Or beta-lactam PLUS fluoroquinolone:
  * Ceftriaxone + levofloxacin

Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia:
Empiric therapy based on local resistance patterns:
- Piperacillin/tazobactam 4.5g q6h or
- Cefepime 2g q8h or
- Meropenem 1g q8h

Add MRSA coverage if risk factors:
- Vancomycin 15-20mg/kg q8-12h or
- Linezolid 600mg q12h

Duration of Therapy:
- CAP: 5-7 days (minimum 5 days)
- HAP/VAP: 7-8 days
- Extend if slow clinical response
- Procalcitonin-guided duration

Supportive Care:
1. Oxygenation:
   - Target oxygen saturation >90%
   - Supplemental oxygen as needed
   - Mechanical ventilation if respiratory failure

2. Fluid Management:
   - Maintain adequate perfusion
   - Avoid volume overload
   - Monitor urine output

3. Pain Management:
   - Analgesics for pleuritic pain
   - Facilitate deep breathing and coughing

4. Nutrition:
   - Maintain adequate nutrition
   - Enteral preferred over parenteral

COMPLICATIONS

Local Complications:
1. Pleural Effusion:
   - Parapneumonic effusion
   - Empyema (infected pleural fluid)
   - May require drainage

2. Lung Abscess:
   - Necrotizing pneumonia
   - Often requires prolonged antibiotics
   - May need drainage

3. Pneumothorax:
   - Spontaneous or iatrogenic
   - May require chest tube

Systemic Complications:
1. Sepsis/Septic Shock:
   - Hemodynamic instability
   - Multi-organ dysfunction
   - High mortality

2. Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):
   - Severe hypoxemia
   - Bilateral infiltrates
   - Low compliance

3. Acute Kidney Injury:
   - Prerenal azotemia
   - Acute tubular necrosis
   - May require dialysis

4. Cardiovascular:
   - Myocardial infarction
   - Arrhythmias
   - Heart failure exacerbation

PREVENTION

Vaccination:
1. Pneumococcal Vaccines:
   - PCV13 (conjugate): <2 years, immunocompromised
   - PPSV23 (polysaccharide): ≥65 years, risk factors
   - Reduces invasive pneumococcal disease

2. Influenza Vaccine:
   - Annual vaccination
   - Reduces secondary bacterial pneumonia
   - All persons ≥6 months

3. COVID-19 Vaccine:
   - Reduces severe disease and pneumonia

Other Preventive Measures:
- Smoking cessation
- Hand hygiene
- Avoid exposure to sick individuals
- Optimal management of chronic diseases
- Adequate nutrition
- Regular exercise

High-Risk Populations:
- Age ≥65 years
- Chronic heart disease
- Chronic lung disease (COPD, asthma)
- Diabetes mellitus
- Chronic kidney disease
- Immunosuppression
- Alcoholism
- Smoking

PROGNOSIS

Factors Associated with Poor Prognosis:
- Advanced age
- Multiple comorbidities
- Delayed treatment
- Severe illness at presentation
- Multidrug-resistant organisms
- Development of complications

Mortality Rates:
- Outpatient CAP: <1%
- Inpatient CAP: 5-15%
- ICU CAP: 20-25%
- HAP/VAP: 20-50%

Recovery:
- Most patients improve within 24-48 hours of appropriate therapy
- Complete resolution may take weeks
- Follow-up chest imaging not routinely needed if clinical improvement

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

Immunocompromised Patients:
- Broader spectrum of pathogens
- Atypical presentations
- Higher mortality
- May require invasive diagnostic procedures
- Longer treatment duration

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease:
- Higher risk of pneumonia
- Often caused by H. influenzae, S. pneumoniae
- May have atypical presentations
- Consider Pseudomonas in severe COPD

Aspiration Pneumonia:
- Risk factors: altered consciousness, dysphagia
- Often polymicrobial
- Anaerobic coverage needed
- Focus on preventing aspiration

Healthcare-Associated Pneumonia:
- Risk factors for MDR organisms:
  * Recent hospitalization
  * Recent antibiotic use
  * Nursing home residence
  * Dialysis
  * Immunosuppression

The management of pneumonia requires prompt recognition, appropriate risk stratification, timely initiation of antibiotics, and supportive care. Prevention through vaccination and management of risk factors is crucial for reducing disease burden.
