Epilepsy

Overview:
Epilepsy is a neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures due to abnormal electrical activity in the brain. It affects people of all ages and can have various underlying causes.

Pathophysiology:
- Abnormal synchronous neuronal firing
- Imbalance between excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission
- Altered ion channel function
- Structural brain abnormalities
- Genetic factors affecting neuronal excitability

Types of Seizures:
Focal Seizures:
Simple Focal (Focal Aware):
- Consciousness maintained
- Motor, sensory, autonomic, or psychic symptoms
- May have aura

Complex Focal (Focal Impaired Awareness):
- Altered consciousness
- Automatisms (lip smacking, picking at clothes)
- Postictal confusion

Focal to Bilateral Tonic-Clonic:
- Begins as focal seizure
- Spreads to involve both hemispheres
- Secondary generalization

Generalized Seizures:
Absence Seizures:
- Brief staring spells (3-30 seconds)
- Sudden onset and offset
- No postictal period
- Common in children

Tonic-Clonic Seizures:
- Tonic phase: muscle stiffening
- Clonic phase: rhythmic jerking
- Loss of consciousness
- Postictal confusion and fatigue

Myoclonic Seizures:
- Brief, shock-like muscle jerks
- Can be isolated or clustered
- Consciousness usually preserved

Atonic Seizures:
- Sudden loss of muscle tone
- "Drop attacks"
- High risk of injury

Clinical Presentation:
Ictal (During Seizure):
- Depends on seizure type and location
- Loss of consciousness (generalized)
- Motor symptoms (jerking, stiffening)
- Sensory symptoms
- Autonomic symptoms (incontinence, flushing)

Postictal (After Seizure):
- Confusion and disorientation
- Fatigue and sleepiness
- Headache
- Muscle soreness
- Todd's paralysis (temporary weakness)

Interictal (Between Seizures):
- May be completely normal
- Subtle cognitive changes
- Mood disorders
- Medication side effects

Etiology:
Idiopathic/Genetic:
- Primary generalized epilepsy
- Genetic epilepsy syndromes
- Family history

Symptomatic/Structural:
- Brain tumors
- Stroke
- Traumatic brain injury
- CNS infections
- Cortical dysplasia
- Hippocampal sclerosis
- Neurodevelopmental disorders

Metabolic:
- Hypoglycemia
- Electrolyte imbalances
- Hypoxia
- Drug withdrawal
- Toxins

Risk Factors:
- Family history of epilepsy
- Head trauma
- Stroke
- CNS infections
- Developmental disorders
- Alcohol abuse
- Sleep deprivation
- Stress

Diagnosis:
Clinical History:
- Detailed seizure description
- Witness accounts
- Triggers and precipitants
- Family history
- Developmental history

Electroencephalogram (EEG):
- Interictal epileptiform discharges
- Ictal patterns during seizures
- Video-EEG monitoring for complex cases
- Sleep-deprived EEG if initial normal

Neuroimaging:
MRI Brain:
- High-resolution structural imaging
- FLAIR, T2, T1 sequences
- Look for structural abnormalities

CT Brain:
- Emergency setting
- Acute trauma or bleeding

Functional Imaging:
- PET scan (interictal hypometabolism)
- SPECT (ictal hyperperfusion)
- For surgical evaluation

Laboratory Tests:
Acute Setting:
- Blood glucose
- Electrolytes (sodium, magnesium, calcium)
- Complete blood count
- Toxicology screen
- Antiepileptic drug levels

Chronic Evaluation:
- Liver function tests
- Kidney function
- Vitamin levels (B12, folate)
- Thyroid function

Differential Diagnosis:
- Syncope
- Psychogenic nonepileptic seizures
- Migraine with aura
- Sleep disorders (parasomnias)
- Movement disorders
- Metabolic disorders
- Cardiac arrhythmias
- Transient ischemic attacks

Treatment:
Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs):
First-Line Agents:
Focal Seizures:
- Levetiracetam
- Lamotrigine
- Carbamazepine
- Oxcarbazepine

Generalized Seizures:
- Valproic acid
- Levetiracetam
- Lamotrigine
- Topiramate

Specific Syndromes:
Absence Seizures:
- Ethosuximide
- Valproic acid
- Lamotrigine

Myoclonic Seizures:
- Valproic acid
- Levetiracetam
- Clonazepam

Drug Selection Considerations:
- Seizure type and syndrome
- Age and gender
- Comorbidities
- Drug interactions
- Side effect profile
- Cost and availability

Surgical Treatment:
Resective Surgery:
- Temporal lobectomy
- Lesionectomy
- Functional hemispherectomy
- For drug-resistant focal epilepsy

Palliative Surgery:
- Corpus callosotomy
- Multiple subpial transections
- For generalized or multifocal epilepsy

Neurostimulation:
- Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS)
- Responsive neurostimulation (RNS)
- Deep brain stimulation (DBS)

Non-Pharmacological Treatment:
Dietary Therapy:
- Ketogenic diet
- Modified Atkins diet
- Low glycemic index treatment
- Particularly effective in children

Lifestyle Modifications:
- Sleep hygiene
- Stress management
- Alcohol avoidance
- Trigger identification and avoidance

Psychosocial Support:
- Counseling and therapy
- Support groups
- Vocational rehabilitation
- Educational accommodations

Complications:
Medical:
- Status epilepticus
- Sudden unexpected death in epilepsy (SUDEP)
- Injuries from seizures
- Medication side effects
- Cognitive impairment

Psychosocial:
- Depression and anxiety
- Social stigma
- Driving restrictions
- Employment limitations
- Relationship difficulties

Status Epilepticus:
Definition:
- Continuous seizure >5 minutes
- Recurrent seizures without recovery

Emergency Management:
- Airway, breathing, circulation
- IV lorazepam or midazolam
- Phenytoin or fosphenytoin
- Consider intubation
- ICU monitoring

Prognosis:
- 70% achieve seizure control with appropriate treatment
- 30% have drug-resistant epilepsy
- Quality of life depends on seizure control
- Most patients can lead normal lives
- SUDEP risk: 1-2 per 1000 patient-years

Special Populations:
Women of Childbearing Age:
- Teratogenic effects of AEDs
- Folic acid supplementation
- Pregnancy planning
- Contraceptive interactions

Elderly:
- Drug interactions
- Reduced clearance
- Comorbidities
- Lower starting doses

Key Points:
- Diagnosis requires at least two unprovoked seizures
- EEG and MRI are essential diagnostic tools
- Treatment aims for seizure freedom with minimal side effects
- Surgical evaluation for drug-resistant epilepsy
- Lifestyle modifications are important adjuncts
- Regular monitoring for medication effects and seizure control
- Patient education about safety and seizure precautions
