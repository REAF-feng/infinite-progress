Stroke (Cerebrovascular Accident): Comprehensive Clinical Management Guide

DEFINITION
Stroke is a clinical syndrome of rapid onset of focal (or global) neurological dysfunction lasting more than 24 hours (or leading to death) with no apparent cause other than vascular origin. It represents the sudden loss of neurological function due to disturbance in blood supply to the brain.

CLASSIFICATION

Ischemic Stroke (85% of all strokes):
1. Large Vessel Atherosclerosis:
   - Carotid, vertebral, or intracranial artery stenosis
   - Accounts for 20% of ischemic strokes

2. Small Vessel Disease (Lacunar):
   - Small penetrating arteries
   - Associated with hypertension and diabetes
   - 25% of ischemic strokes

3. Cardioembolic:
   - Atrial fibrillation most common cause
   - Heart failure, valve disease, recent MI
   - 30% of ischemic strokes

4. Other Determined Etiology:
   - Arterial dissection
   - Vasculitis
   - Hypercoagulable states
   - 5% of strokes

5. Undetermined Etiology (Cryptogenic):
   - Multiple potential causes
   - No clear single cause
   - 20% of strokes

Hemorrhagic Stroke (15% of all strokes):
1. Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH):
   - Bleeding within brain parenchyma
   - 10% of all strokes
   - Higher mortality than ischemic

2. Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):
   - Bleeding into subarachnoid space
   - 5% of all strokes
   - Often due to aneurysm rupture

Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA):
- Focal neurological symptoms <24 hours
- Usually <1 hour duration
- No permanent brain damage
- High risk for subsequent stroke

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- Leading cause of disability worldwide
- Second leading cause of death globally
- ~795,000 strokes annually in US
- ~610,000 are first-time strokes
- Stroke incidence increases with age
- More common in men <75, women >75
- Higher incidence in African Americans
- Geographic variation ("stroke belt" in southeastern US)

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

Ischemic Stroke:
1. Thrombotic:
   - In-situ clot formation
   - Usually on atherosclerotic plaque
   - Gradual onset common

2. Embolic:
   - Clot travels from distant source
   - Sudden onset typical
   - Often cardiac source

3. Hypoperfusion:
   - Systemic hypotension
   - Watershed territory infarcts
   - Usually bilateral

Ischemic Cascade:
- Energy failure → membrane depolarization
- Calcium influx → cellular toxicity
- Free radical formation → cell death
- Inflammatory response → tissue damage
- Penumbra: tissue at risk but salvageable

Hemorrhagic Stroke:
- Direct mass effect
- Increased intracranial pressure
- Blood breakdown products toxicity
- Surrounding edema

RISK FACTORS

Non-Modifiable:
- Age (risk doubles each decade after 55)
- Gender (men higher risk <75 years)
- Race/ethnicity (African Americans highest risk)
- Family history
- Prior stroke or TIA

Modifiable:
1. Cardiovascular:
   - Hypertension (most important)
   - Atrial fibrillation
   - Coronary artery disease
   - Heart failure
   - Valvular heart disease

2. Metabolic:
   - Diabetes mellitus
   - Dyslipidemia
   - Obesity
   - Metabolic syndrome

3. Lifestyle:
   - Smoking (increases risk 2-4x)
   - Excessive alcohol use
   - Physical inactivity
   - Poor diet

4. Other:
   - Carotid artery stenosis
   - Sleep apnea
   - Oral contraceptive use
   - Hormone replacement therapy
   - Drug use (cocaine, amphetamines)

CLINICAL PRESENTATION

Sudden Onset Neurological Deficits:

Anterior Circulation (Carotid Territory):
1. Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA):
   - Contralateral hemiparesis/hemiplegia
   - Contralateral hemisensory loss
   - Aphasia (dominant hemisphere)
   - Neglect (non-dominant hemisphere)
   - Gaze deviation toward lesion
   - Homonymous hemianopia

2. Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA):
   - Contralateral leg weakness
   - Abulia (apathy)
   - Urinary incontinence
   - Gait apraxia

Posterior Circulation (Vertebrobasilar):
1. Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA):
   - Homonymous hemianopia
   - Alexia without agraphia
   - Memory impairment

2. Vertebrobasilar:
   - Diplopia, vertigo, nausea
   - Ataxia, dysarthria
   - Dysphagia
   - Crossed sensory/motor signs
   - Coma (brainstem involvement)

Lacunar Syndromes:
1. Pure Motor Hemiparesis
2. Pure Sensory Stroke
3. Ataxic Hemiparesis
4. Dysarthria-Clumsy Hand Syndrome

Hemorrhagic Stroke:
- Severe headache
- Nausea and vomiting
- Decreased consciousness
- Focal neurological deficits
- Neck stiffness (SAH)

Warning Signs (FAST):
- Face drooping
- Arm weakness
- Speech difficulty
- Time to call emergency services

DIAGNOSTIC EVALUATION

Immediate Assessment:
1. Time of symptom onset
2. Current medications (especially anticoagulants)
3. Recent surgery or trauma
4. NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS)

Neuroimaging:

Non-Contrast CT Head:
- First-line imaging
- Excludes hemorrhage
- May be normal in acute ischemic stroke
- Shows early signs of infarction

CT Angiography (CTA):
- Evaluates large vessel occlusion
- Guide endovascular therapy
- Assess for carotid stenosis

MRI Brain:
- More sensitive for acute infarction
- DWI shows acute ischemia
- FLAIR rules out stroke mimics
- Better for posterior circulation

MR Angiography (MRA):
- Non-invasive vessel imaging
- No contrast needed
- Limited resolution compared to CTA

CT/MR Perfusion:
- Assesses tissue at risk
- Guides extended time window therapy
- Identifies penumbra

Laboratory Tests:
- Complete blood count
- Basic metabolic panel
- PT/INR, aPTT
- Glucose
- Troponin
- Toxicology screen (if indicated)

Cardiac Evaluation:
- ECG (atrial fibrillation, acute MI)
- Echocardiogram (cardioembolic source)
- Cardiac monitoring (24-48 hours)

Vascular Imaging:
- Carotid ultrasound
- Conventional angiography (if intervention planned)
- CT/MR angiography of neck

ACUTE TREATMENT

Ischemic Stroke:

IV Thrombolysis (tPA):
Eligibility Criteria:
- Symptom onset <4.5 hours
- Age 18-80 years (relative)
- NIHSS score typically >4
- No contraindications

Contraindications:
- Recent stroke, trauma, or surgery
- Active bleeding
- Anticoagulation with elevated INR
- Severe hypertension (>185/110)
- Rapidly improving symptoms

Dose: 0.9 mg/kg (max 90mg)
- 10% as bolus
- 90% as infusion over 60 minutes

Mechanical Thrombectomy:
Indications:
- Large vessel occlusion (LVO)
- Time window up to 24 hours (selected patients)
- NIHSS ≥6
- Good functional status pre-stroke

Procedures:
- Stent retrievers
- Aspiration catheters
- Combined techniques

Extended Time Windows:
- 4.5-9 hours: Perfusion imaging guided
- 6-24 hours: Clinical-core mismatch
- Wake-up stroke: MRI guided

Blood Pressure Management:
- Target <185/110 before tPA
- <180/105 after tPA
- Avoid precipitous drops

Hemorrhagic Stroke:

Intracerebral Hemorrhage:
1. Blood Pressure Control:
   - Target SBP 140-179 mmHg
   - Nicardipine, clevidipine preferred
   - Avoid sublingual nifedipine

2. Hemostasis:
   - Reverse anticoagulation
   - Vitamin K, PCC, FFP
   - Platelet transfusion if severe thrombocytopenia

3. Intracranial Pressure:
   - Monitor if GCS <8
   - Mannitol, hypertonic saline
   - Surgical evacuation (selected cases)

Subarachnoid Hemorrhage:
1. Aneurysm Securing:
   - Surgical clipping
   - Endovascular coiling
   - Within 24-72 hours

2. Vasospasm Prevention:
   - Nimodipine 60mg q4h × 21 days
   - Maintain euvolemia
   - Avoid hypotension

SECONDARY PREVENTION

Antiplatelet Therapy:
1. Aspirin:
   - 75-100mg daily
   - First-line for most patients
   - Start within 24-48 hours

2. Clopidogrel:
   - 75mg daily
   - Alternative to aspirin
   - May be superior in certain populations

3. Dual Antiplatelet Therapy:
   - Aspirin + clopidogrel
   - Short-term use (21-90 days)
   - Minor stroke or TIA

Anticoagulation:
Indications:
- Atrial fibrillation
- Mechanical heart valves
- Cardioembolic source

Agents:
- Warfarin (target INR 2-3)
- DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran)
- Start 1-2 weeks after stroke

Risk Factors Modification:

Blood Pressure:
- Target <130/80 mmHg
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs preferred
- Combination therapy often needed

Cholesterol:
- High-intensity statin therapy
- Target LDL <70 mg/dL
- Consider PCSK9 inhibitors

Diabetes:
- HbA1c <7%
- Avoid hypoglycemia
- Consider SGLT2 inhibitors

Lifestyle:
- Smoking cessation
- Mediterranean diet
- Regular exercise
- Weight management
- Alcohol moderation

Carotid Intervention:
- Carotid endarterectomy
- Carotid artery stenting
- >50% stenosis symptomatic
- >70% stenosis asymptomatic

COMPLICATIONS

Acute Complications:
1. Cerebral Edema:
   - Peaks 2-5 days
   - May require decompressive craniectomy
   - Monitor with serial CT

2. Hemorrhagic Transformation:
   - 6-15% of ischemic strokes
   - Higher risk with large infarcts
   - Symptomatic in 2-3%

3. Seizures:
   - 5-10% of strokes
   - Higher with cortical involvement
   - Prophylaxis not recommended

4. Medical Complications:
   - Pneumonia (aspiration)
   - DVT/PE
   - UTI
   - Pressure ulcers

Long-term Complications:
1. Post-stroke Depression:
   - 30-50% of patients
   - Impacts rehabilitation
   - Treat with SSRIs

2. Cognitive Impairment:
   - Vascular dementia
   - Executive dysfunction
   - Memory problems

3. Spasticity:
   - Affects 20-40% of patients
   - Baclofen, tizanidine
   - Botulinum toxin injections

REHABILITATION

Acute Rehabilitation:
- Early mobilization
- Swallowing assessment
- Speech therapy
- Physical therapy
- Occupational therapy

Goals:
- Maximize functional independence
- Prevent complications
- Facilitate community reintegration
- Family education and support

Multidisciplinary Team:
- Physicians
- Nurses
- Physical therapists
- Occupational therapists
- Speech-language pathologists
- Social workers
- Neuropsychologists

PROGNOSIS

Factors Affecting Outcome:
- Stroke severity (NIHSS)
- Age
- Pre-stroke functional status
- Stroke location and size
- Time to treatment
- Comorbidities

Functional Outcomes:
- 50-70% achieve functional independence
- 15-30% have severe disability
- 10-15% require institutional care

Mortality:
- 30-day mortality: 10-15%
- 1-year mortality: 20-25%
- Hemorrhagic stroke higher mortality

Recurrence Risk:
- 3-4% in first 30 days
- 10-15% in first year
- Highest risk in first 90 days
- Secondary prevention reduces risk by 20-25%

SPECIAL POPULATIONS

Young Stroke:
- Age <45 years
- Different etiologies (dissection, vasculitis)
- Extensive workup needed
- Focus on underlying cause

Pregnancy:
- 30 per 100,000 deliveries
- Peripartum period highest risk
- Avoid ACE inhibitors, statins
- Aspirin safe in pregnancy

Elderly:
- Higher stroke incidence
- More complications
- Careful consideration of risks/benefits
- Quality of life considerations

The management of stroke requires rapid recognition, immediate treatment, comprehensive care, and long-term prevention strategies. Time-sensitive interventions and multidisciplinary care are essential for optimal outcomes.
