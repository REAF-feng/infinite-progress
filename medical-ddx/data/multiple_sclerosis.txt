Multiple Sclerosis

Overview:
Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a chronic autoimmune demyelinating disease of the central nervous system, characterized by inflammation, demyelination, and neurodegeneration. It affects young adults and can cause significant disability.

Pathophysiology:
- Autoimmune attack on myelin sheaths
- T-cell mediated inflammatory response
- Blood-brain barrier disruption
- Oligodendrocyte damage and loss
- Axonal damage and neurodegeneration
- Formation of sclerotic plaques in white matter

Types of Multiple Sclerosis:
Relapsing-Remitting MS (RRMS):
- 85% of patients at onset
- Discrete relapses followed by recovery
- Stable disability between relapses
- Most common form in young adults

Secondary Progressive MS (SPMS):
- Evolution from RRMS
- Gradual worsening with or without relapses
- Accumulating disability over time
- Usually occurs 10-20 years after onset

Primary Progressive MS (PPMS):
- 10-15% of patients
- Gradual worsening from onset
- No clear relapses or remissions
- Typically older age at onset

Progressive-Relapsing MS (PRMS):
- Rare form (<5%)
- Progressive course with acute relapses
- Continuous worsening between relapses

Clinical Presentation:
Common Initial Symptoms:
- Optic neuritis (vision loss, eye pain)
- Sensory symptoms (numbness, tingling)
- Motor weakness
- Brainstem symptoms (diplopia, vertigo)
- Cerebellar symptoms (ataxia, tremor)

Motor Symptoms:
- Weakness (hemiparesis, paraparesis)
- Spasticity and stiffness
- Fatigue
- Gait difficulties
- Coordination problems

Sensory Symptoms:
- Numbness and tingling
- Burning sensations
- Loss of vibration/position sense
- Lhermitte's sign (electric shock sensation)
- Trigeminal neuralgia

Visual Symptoms:
- Optic neuritis
- Diplopia
- Nystagmus
- Visual field defects
- Internuclear ophthalmoplegia

Cognitive Symptoms:
- Memory problems
- Executive dysfunction
- Processing speed reduction
- Attention difficulties
- Word-finding problems

Bladder and Bowel:
- Urinary urgency and frequency
- Incontinence
- Retention
- Constipation

Other Symptoms:
- Depression and anxiety
- Sexual dysfunction
- Sleep disorders
- Heat sensitivity (Uhthoff's phenomenon)
- Pseudobulbar affect

Risk Factors:
- Age (typically 20-40 years)
- Female gender (3:1 ratio)
- Northern latitude/vitamin D deficiency
- Genetic factors (HLA-DRB1*15:01)
- Smoking
- Epstein-Barr virus infection
- Obesity in adolescence

Diagnosis:
McDonald Criteria (2017):
Requires evidence of:
- Dissemination in space (multiple CNS locations)
- Dissemination in time (lesions of different ages)
- No better explanation for clinical picture

MRI Findings:
T2/FLAIR Lesions:
- Periventricular
- Cortical/juxtacortical
- Infratentorial
- Spinal cord

Gadolinium Enhancement:
- Active inflammation
- Acute lesions
- Evidence of disease activity

Oligoclonal Bands:
- CSF-specific oligoclonal bands
- IgG synthesis in CNS
- Present in 85-95% of MS patients

Evoked Potentials:
- Visual evoked potentials
- Somatosensory evoked potentials
- Brainstem auditory evoked potentials
- Detect subclinical lesions

Differential Diagnosis:
- Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder
- Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis
- CNS vasculitis
- Sarcoidosis
- Behcet's disease
- Lyme disease
- Vitamin B12 deficiency
- Mitochondrial disorders

Treatment:
Disease-Modifying Therapies (DMTs):
Injectable Therapies:
- Interferon beta-1a (Avonex, Rebif)
- Interferon beta-1b (Betaseron)
- Glatiramer acetate (Copaxone)
- First-line therapies with moderate efficacy

Oral Therapies:
- Fingolimod (Gilenya)
- Dimethyl fumarate (Tecfidera)
- Teriflunomide (Aubagio)
- Cladribine (Mavenclad)
- Ozanimod (Zeposia)
- Ponesimod (Ponvory)

Infusion Therapies:
- Natalizumab (Tysabri) - high efficacy, PML risk
- Alemtuzumab (Lemtrada) - high efficacy, safety concerns
- Ocrelizumab (Ocrevus) - approved for RRMS and PPMS
- Rituximab (off-label)

Treatment Selection:
- Disease activity and severity
- Patient preferences and lifestyle
- Safety profile and monitoring requirements
- Pregnancy plans
- Comorbidities

Acute Relapse Treatment:
- High-dose corticosteroids (methylprednisolone)
- Plasma exchange for severe relapses
- Supportive care

Symptomatic Treatment:
Spasticity:
- Baclofen
- Tizanidine
- Botulinum toxin
- Intrathecal baclofen pump

Fatigue:
- Amantadine
- Modafinil
- Energy conservation
- Exercise programs

Neuropathic Pain:
- Gabapentin
- Pregabalin
- Tricyclic antidepressants
- Duloxetine

Bladder Dysfunction:
- Anticholinergics
- Beta-3 agonists
- Intermittent catheterization
- Botulinum toxin

Depression:
- SSRIs
- SNRIs
- Cognitive behavioral therapy
- Support groups

Rehabilitation:
Physical Therapy:
- Gait training
- Strengthening exercises
- Balance training
- Spasticity management

Occupational Therapy:
- Activities of daily living
- Adaptive equipment
- Fatigue management
- Cognitive rehabilitation

Speech Therapy:
- Swallowing assessment
- Communication strategies
- Cognitive-communication therapy

Monitoring and Follow-up:
Clinical Assessment:
- EDSS (Expanded Disability Status Scale)
- Relapse rate
- Disease progression
- Quality of life measures

MRI Monitoring:
- Annual brain MRI
- New or enlarging T2 lesions
- Gadolinium-enhancing lesions
- Brain atrophy measurements

Laboratory Monitoring:
- Complete blood count
- Liver function tests
- JCV antibody status
- Vitamin D levels

Prognosis:
Favorable Factors:
- Younger age at onset
- Female gender
- Relapsing-remitting course
- Complete recovery from relapses
- Longer time to second relapse

Poor Prognostic Factors:
- Male gender
- Older age at onset
- Progressive course from onset
- Motor symptoms at onset
- Multiple relapses in first year

Long-term Outcomes:
- 50% reach EDSS 6 (walking aid) in 15-20 years
- 10% have benign course
- Life expectancy reduced by 5-10 years
- Quality of life significantly affected

Pregnancy and MS:
- Relapse rate decreases during pregnancy
- Increased relapse risk postpartum
- Most DMTs discontinued during pregnancy
- Breastfeeding considerations
- Genetic counseling

Key Points:
- Chronic autoimmune demyelinating disease
- Diagnosis requires clinical and MRI evidence
- Early treatment with DMTs improves outcomes
- Multidisciplinary approach essential
- Regular monitoring for disease activity
- Symptomatic treatment improves quality of life
- Pregnancy planning requires special considerations
